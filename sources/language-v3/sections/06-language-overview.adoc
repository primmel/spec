[[language-overview]]
== Language overview

=== The modelling claim

A Primmel model expresses what a standard says (reference content), what a
conforming subject is, has, and does (subject content), how conformity is
checked (operations and evidence), and how conformity is established and
judged (workflow and verdicts), in one language. Four consequences
organize everything else:

* secondary models anchor to the subject and own no subject facts (a
requirement binds aspect paths; it never restates them);
* facts and judgments are separate tiers (a test report contains no
verdicts; a verdict is a re-runnable function over definitions and facts);
* growth happens inside the semantic sorts (new kinds, properties, values,
transitions), never beside them;
* the document of the standard is a reconstruction from the model, not the
source of it.

[[semantic-basis]]
=== The semantic basis: the eight terms, LutaML, and MMEL

Primmel's semantics is not invented here and not inherited whole from
one language. It has three layers, in this order.

==== The ground: the eight terms

The semantic ground is the algebra of the foundation volume (the
_IS–HAS–DOES modelling system_, normatively cited): eight terms —
three relations and five sorts — with three operations and three
closure rules.

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Term |Meaning

|`IS` |individuation: _x is the same entity as y_, and _x is a K_ — one relation, two grains
|`HAS` |attribution: an object has a value along a property
|`DOES` |dynamics: an object does a transition
|`O` objects |the bearers of all claims
|`P` properties |the slots along which objects vary
|`V` values |what fills a property slot
|`T` transitions |input → transform → output rules
|`PROCESS = ρ(T) ⊆ O` |reified transitions: a step is a transition an object does; a process is their composition
|===

The operations are composition (`∘`, transitions compose when their
interfaces match), reification (`ρ`, a transition becomes an object),
and embedding (`ι`, an object can be a value). The closure rules seal
the system: **kinds are objects** (the codomain of IS is O — no TYPE
primitive; the power-type discipline of <<power-type-instantiation,9.4>>
is this rule's expression), **values hold references** (every relation
between objects is a property whose value is an object-reference — no
RELATION primitive), and **process is reified transition** (the
dynamic axis folds into the static one; one machinery serves both).

The foundation volume proves the system closed (nothing escapes the
eight terms), complete (every descriptive claim factors through them),
and extensible (it grows by content, never by new primitives). Every
construct of this specification is a composition over these terms;
a construct that cannot be read as one is a defect of this
specification.

==== The surface and the vocabulary

On that ground, Primmel INHERITS two:

* **LutaML** (the LutaML Language Reference, the pinned edition —
  normatively cited in <<normative-references>>) is the data-modeling
  SURFACE — the syntax of the object/property/value axis: packages, classes, attributes (name, type, definition,
  cardinality, value restriction), enumerations, instances in the
  assignment form, references and inclusion, value types, and the
  serialization mappings. Every data-bearing construct in this
  specification is LutaML's, in one of three declared modes:
  **inherited** (the construct IS LutaML's; the clause cites the
  source), **refined** (LutaML's semantics hold with a declared
  refinement), or **extended** (no LutaML counterpart; the rationale
  is stated).
* **MMEL** (the MMEL element semantics — the reconciled parts:
  data model, forms, symbols, calculations, state machines, process
  model, measurement, terminology, compliance) is the element basis:
  forms and their binding, symbols and their units, calculations and
  their typed inputs/outputs and expression discipline, state
  machines and their transitions and guards, the process dialect,
  the measurement types, term entries, and provisions with their
  modality. The same three modes apply, part by part.

What Primmel ADDS — and the only places where this specification
creates vocabulary rather than citing it — is the SMART layer, each
piece a named composition over the eight terms:
the subject anatomies and the definition/instance discipline, the
evidence plane (attribute definitions with scope and origin,
provenance, attestations and the certificate lineage), the validation
plane (verdicts and their chains, conformance tests and their
executable programs), the quantity and duality discipline, the
power-type modelling relationships, and the composition and edition
machinery of packages. Annex A records, for every construct, its
basis and mode; a construct without a stated basis is a defect of
this specification.

The discipline the basis imposes: where LutaML or MMEL defines the
semantics, this specification cites and conforms — it never
restates in different words and never drifts; where Primmel extends,
the extension states what it extends and why. The conformance suite
carries the same discipline: an inherited construct's fixtures are
the basis language's fixtures where they exist.

[[tier-system]]
=== The tier system

Every model element lives on exactly one tier, and dependencies point only
upward:

[cols="1,2,2"]
|===
|Tier |What lives there |Examples

|Foundations
|What everything references; anchored to nothing in the subject
|Vocabulary registers, parties and roles, units and value types, time
primitives

|Primary
|The subject and its aspects
|The instrument chain, attribute definitions, behaviors, condition sets,
state

|Secondary
|Models anchored to primary aspect paths
|Requirements (constraints), conformance tests (operations), forms
(evidence views)

|Tertiary
|Execution and judgment over secondary × primary instances
|Runs and evidence records, verdicts, evaluations, decisions,
certificates, workflow processes, monitors, dataspaces and their usage
policies

|Cross-cutting
|Properties of every element on every tier
|Provenance, time and versioning, the traceability spine,
multilinguality, correspondence annotations
|===

A downward reference is a modelling error, and the checker reports one
where the dependency is machine-visible (<<annex-b-rule-catalog,Annex
B>>). The tier law has two named consequences: the *anchoring rule*
(secondary models bind subject facts, they never restate them) and the
*fact/judgment firewall* (evidence contains no verdicts).

[[package-kinds]]
=== Packages and the five package kinds

The unit of publication and reuse is the *package*: one directory holding a
manifest and content files (<<package-layout,16.3>>). A manifest declares
exactly one package kind when it declares one:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Kind |Meaning

|`core`
|The shared kernel: vocabulary, parties, units, value types; composed into
other packages, anchored to no Recommendation

|`module`
|A shared capability package consumed by two or more Recommendation
packages

|`rec`
|A publishable Recommendation: the reference model of one standard

|`product_reference`
|A manufacturer's product model, related to a Recommendation by mapping
only (never a refinement of the standard); carries `manufacturer`,
`product`, and `maps_to` facets, and is imported by pinned abstract import
(`uses { acme-lc500@2021 }`, C81–C83)

|`certification_program`
|A scheme operator's certification program: a fourth publisher with the
product-reference shape, related to Recommendations (`maps_to`) and to
product packages (pinned abstract imports) by mapping only, composed into
nothing (C97, C98)
|===

NOTE: The kind facet is optional in the grammar; a manifest without one is
an ordinary (rec) package. The five kinds are the closed enumeration the
parser accepts (`PackageKind` in `packages/primmel/src/types/Package.ts`).

[[artifact-kinds]]
=== Artifact kinds

A Primmel workspace is made of the following artifact kinds:

[cols="1,1,3"]
|===
|Extension |Kind |Purpose

|`.prl`
|file
|A model text: the core artifact. Plain text, UTF-8. The `.mmel`
extension is the legacy spelling, accepted everywhere `.prl` is
(<<annex-d-lineage,Annex D>>).

|`package.primmel`
|file
|The package manifest: the only required file of a package
(<<package-manifest,9.3>>).

|`.prm`
|file
|A Primmel Map: a JSON mapping between two models, versioned independently
of the models it links (<<prm-format,16.4>>).

|`.prd`
|payload
|Clause-level extracts of a source standard, consumed by the text-coverage
audit as sentence payloads (<<coverage-audits,17.5>>). Consumer-side
format; informative here.

|`.pws/`
|directory
|A Primmel Workspace: runtime records produced when a model runs.
Consumer-side format; informative here.
|===

[[reference-implementation-models]]
=== Reference models, implementation models, and mapping

A standards publisher publishes a reference model. An adopting organization
publishes an implementation model. A *mapping* connects the two, element by
element, expressed either in-model (`map_profile`,
<<map-profiles,12.8>>) or as a standalone `.prm` file
(<<prm-format,16.4>>); the two serializations are equivalent and bridge
into one model form. The mapping coverage calculus (full, minimal, partial,
none) is computed per target namespace, and authored coverage assertions
are checked against the computed calculus (C23). A namespace that is
imported into a package (`uses`) MUST NOT also be the target of a mapping
from that package (C24, import-not-mapping): import is structural
inclusion, mapping is the compliance relation, and the two are disjoint.

[[composition-rules]]
=== Package composition

A package composes other packages structurally with `uses` (the deprecated
single `extends` is read as a one-element `uses`). Composition semantics:

. the dependency closure is merged in topological order, dependencies
first, the root package last;
. a downstream package may reference upstream identifiers but MUST NOT
redefine them (C28, no-redefine);
. the import graph MUST be acyclic (C29);
. every import MUST resolve through the package locator in use (C27);
. capability tokens (`provides`, `requires`) are matched across the
closure: an unsatisfied `requires` is an error (C31); a `provides` that
nothing consumes is a warning unless waived (C30);
. a version-pinned entry `id@edition` is an abstract import of a product
reference package: located and checked (C83), never content-merged.
. a requirement namespace — a requirement class identifier path, e.g.
`/req/cs` — declared by a composed package is OWNED by that package
(<<requirements-and-conformance,clause 11>>): a downstream package may
reference its provisions but MUST NOT declare a requirement class or a
requirement whose identifier sits at or under an owned namespace (C119,
namespace-pin-violation; the exact-identifier case is C28).

Editions are the package lifecycle: a manifest registers its `editions`,
the lifecycle `status` (`current`, `preview`, `superseded`, `withdrawn`),
the `validity` window, and the `supersedes`/`replaces`/`superseded_by`
lineage edges, all checked by the edition rules (C77–C80, C85, C113;
<<edition-lineage,9.3.1>>). The structural model diff between two
package states is a toolchain operation (`primmel diff`,
<<diff-and-exports,17.6>>), not a language construct.
